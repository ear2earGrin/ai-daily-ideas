## Concept

Sell a **same-day automation quote-gap card** when a freelancer or indie automation seller is about to send a proposal, and the number on the page is a tool plan or a 90-day ROI line.

The customer sends:

- the draft quote or proposal (price, scope, promised payback, what is included).
- a short process note: the job they are automating, in their words or the client's (how a booking, a quote, or an invoice gets done today). A bullet list is enough. A 17-step paste is better.
- optional: the tool stack named in the quote (n8n, Make, Zapier, a voice agent, a model API) and one exception they already know about.
- optional: one prior job where the monthly fee did not cover the second month.

Agents plus one human operator return within one business day:

1. A price split: tool plan, setup, human review, and "not priced" for anything the draft names but does not cost.
2. A five-bucket gap list, each tagged `in the quote`, `named but unpriced`, or `not in the quote`: setup and connector work, exception loops, human review after the office closes, connector or selector maintenance, and model retries or overnight token burn.
3. A payback line: the quote's claimed window next to a range the materials can support (`90 days claimed / not-in-record`, or `narrow job, 6–12 months if exceptions stay at one`). No invented savings.
4. A retainer sentence: the one monthly line that covers the bucket most likely to show up in month two (a retry cap, an exception desk, or a session-restart check). If the work cannot justify a retainer, say so.
5. A paste-ready footnote the seller can drop under the price before they send it.
6. A source appendix. No claim that the client's process was audited, that payback is guaranteed, or that the agent will stay up after the office closes.

This is **not** a process-mining engagement, **not** a live spend cap, and **not** the automation build. It is a productized desk that makes a draft quote inspectable before the client signs the tool plan.

## Target user

- Primary buyer: EU/UK/US freelance automation sellers and indie hackers quoting $15–50/month tool plans, or a fixed setup plus a thin retainer, for clinics, local shops, and 1–5 person service businesses.
- Urgent pain: the proposal looks cheap because the booking flow is "already set up." The second month is the exception loop, the closed-office session, or a retry bill the quote never named. The seller either eats it or loses the client.
- Existing workaround: send the tool price, hope the retainer conversation happens later, or spend two weeks interviewing before a quote the client will not pay for.

## Execution plan (one person + agents)

**Agent 1 — Intake.** Accept a quote paste plus a process note. Refuse card numbers, national IDs, and medical files.

**Agent 2 — Quote clerk.** List the price, the included work, the claimed payback, and every cost the draft does not name. Do not upgrade a tool plan into a full delivery price.

**Agent 3 — Bucket clerk.** Tag setup, exceptions, human review, connector maintenance, and retries as `in the quote`, `named but unpriced`, or `not in the quote`. Quote the note or write `not-in-record`.

**Agent 4 — Footnote writer.** Write the payback line and the retainer sentence. Default: do not promise 90 days if the note still has an exception loop and no owner for it.

**Agent 5 — Pack writer.** Price split, gap list, payback line, retainer sentence, paste footnote, appendix.

**Human operator.** Delete invented savings. Export Markdown + PDF. The model does not email the seller's client.

Stack to start: one LLM, a paste inbox, a Stripe Payment Link, a mailbox. No n8n login and no vendor console in week one.

## Risks and mitigations

- Fake ROI: if the note has no volumes, do not invent "$31 to $6." Write `not-in-record` and keep the payback line as a range the seller must not promise.
- Pretending to audit the client: the pack is a research note on the files the seller sent. Do not say the process was mined or that payback is guaranteed.
- Overlap with scope-change-order desk (2026-09-04): that pack is after signature, when the client adds work. This pack is before send, when the quote itself is thin.
- Overlap with spend attribution (2026-09-21): that pack maps a landed invoice to jobs and loops. This pack prices the retry bucket before the quote goes out. Sell both; do not merge the files.
- Overlap with vendor renewal desk (2026-09-10): that pack catches a renewal notice already in the inbox. This pack is the first quote.
- Overlap with judgment-rule harvest (2026-09-16): that pack writes the checklist. This pack only prices the line that says the checklist is included.
- Overlap with why-not-working diagnosis (2026-09-17): that pack explains a live workflow that already failed. This pack is a draft that has not been sent.
- Session claims: if the seller promises the agent runs after the office closes and the note has no restart owner, mark it `not in the quote`. Do not certify uptime.
- Channel terms: do not log into the client's n8n, Make, or model console. The seller pastes.
