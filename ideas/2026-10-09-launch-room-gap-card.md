## Concept

Sell a **launch-room gap card** to a solo builder who has a working product and almost no one using it.

The customer sends:

- the product URL, a one-paragraph description, and the price they hope to charge.
- the one room they have already posted in (X account, Discord, a launch page, or "nowhere yet").
- optional: three competitor URLs, or "pick them."
- optional: one post they are proud of and one post that got no replies.

Agents plus one human operator return within one business day:

1. A room map: up to five public rooms where the job is already discussed (X search, a public forum thread, a launch page, an answer-engine snippet). Each room gets a one-line buyer quote or `not-found`.
2. A gap line: where the builder is posting versus where the complaint already lives. Name the mismatch in one sentence.
3. A wrong-room skip: one channel they should stop feeding this week, with the evidence (no buyer language, only other builders, or a launchpad that takes a fee before a user exists).
4. A 7-day insertion pack: seven drafts in their voice, each tied to one real thread or account, plus the reply they should not send.
5. One pricing test: a single offer (free audit, 29 EUR founding price, or a paid concierge week) written so it can be posted in the right room, not a feature list.
6. A source appendix. No claim that a guild, a token, or an algorithm will distribute the product.

This is **not** a community manager, **not** a token launch, and **not** a content calendar for an empty account. It is a productized desk that makes the first distribution bet inspectable.

## Target user

- Primary buyer: a solo vibe-coder or indie hacker with a shipped URL and fewer than 20 paying or active users.
- Urgent pain: building is no longer the bottleneck. Posting into builder rooms does not produce a buyer, and a launchpad fee is not a community.
- Existing workaround: another feature, a build-in-public thread, or paying a launchpad before anyone has asked for the job.

## Why this works

- Market signal: the 9 Oct posts name community and monetization as the pain after the product exists. The 8 Oct indie-hacker thread says 1x shipping is table stakes. Agent marketing teams are already being open-sourced, so the scarce object is the room, not the agent.
- Agent advantage: search public posts, quote buyer language, refuse a room that only contains other builders, draft seven insertions tied to real threads.
- Solo-operator advantage: one person can tell a builder circle from a buyer circle. The model drafts. The operator deletes any draft that would only be liked by other founders.

## Monetization

- Primary model: per-card service, plus a weekly retainer if they want the next seven insertions after they post the first pack.
- First price test: 89 EUR for one product, five rooms, and a 7-day pack, delivered next business day. 149 EUR if the operator also records which of the seven posts actually got a buyer reply. 39 EUR for a builder with no URL yet: room map only, no drafts.
- Upsell: 49 EUR to rescore after they change the price; 199 EUR/month for a weekly gap card on the same product. The desk does not post from their account in week one.
- Fun / public variant: run the card on one of your own shipped tools and publish the wrong-room line. That sample is the ad.

## Validation plan

1. Riskiest assumption: a vibe-coder with a live URL will pay ~89 EUR for a cited room card instead of posting another build thread.
2. Demand test: publish one sample card on 9–11 Oct. Reply to 15 posts from people who shipped something and said community or monetization is the hard part. Offer the first 5 cards at 39 EUR, paid before the URL is opened.
3. Success bar: 5 serious replies and 2 paid cards in 14 days. Kill if 15 touches produce zero deposits.

## Execution plan (one person + agents)

**Agent 1 — Intake.** Accept a URL, a price, and the room they already use. Refuse private DMs, customer lists, and anything that requires logging into their X account.

**Agent 2 — Room clerk.** Search public X and the open web for the job, not the product name. Quote one buyer sentence per room. If a room only has builders, label it `builder-only`.

**Agent 3 — Competitor clerk.** Read the three competitor pages or pick three public ones. Note the price and the room they actually send people to. `not-on-page` if the destination is missing.

**Agent 4 — Pack writer.** Gap line, skip line, seven drafts tied to real threads, one pricing test. No invented engagement numbers.

**Agent 5 — Pack assembler.** Card, drafts, appendix.

**Human operator.** Delete any draft aimed at other founders. Export Markdown. The model does not post, reply, or join a guild on the customer's behalf in week one.

Stack to start: one LLM, X and web search, a paste inbox, a Stripe Payment Link. No posting token in week one.

## First 7-day action plan

1. Pin the evidence standard: a room counts only with a public buyer sentence, and a draft counts only if it points at a real thread.
2. Run the card on one of your own URLs and publish the wrong-room line.
3. Put 89 EUR on a one-pager.
4. Send 15 replies to vibe-coder and indie-hacker posts about community or monetization.
5. Run two paid pilots.
6. Time human review. Target under 40 minutes after card two.
7. Keep or kill.

## Risks and mitigations

- Builder echo: if every quote is from other founders, say so and do not dress it up as demand.
- Launchpad bias: a guild or token room is a room, not proof of users. Label fee-before-user rooms as skips unless a buyer is already there.
- Overlap with the floor-language menu (2026-10-09): that pack is for a shop owner deciding whether to buy an agent. This pack is for a builder deciding where to talk. Do not merge the files.
- Overlap with the ranking-signal posting card (2026-10-08): that pack scores a post already written. This pack picks the room before the post.
- Account safety: do not take the customer's X login in week one.
- ToS: public posts and public pages only.
