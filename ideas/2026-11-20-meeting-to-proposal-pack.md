## Concept

Sell a **same-week (or same-day) meeting-to-proposal pack** when a freelancer or boutique agency has just finished a discovery call or email exchange and needs a professional proposal out before the client’s attention fades.

The customer pastes or uploads:

- meeting notes, transcript, voice-note summary, or email thread
- any existing rate card, past SOW, or package description
- optional: brand voice notes, must-include clauses, or the client’s stated budget

Agents plus one human operator return within 24 hours (or 4–8 hours on rush):

1. A one-page scope matrix: every stated requirement tagged `included / optional / out-of-scope / needs-clarification` with a source quote.
2. A clean proposal letter or one-pager in the freelancer’s voice.
3. A numbered SOW with deliverables, assumptions, exclusions, and timeline.
4. A quote table (fixed, hourly, or hybrid) that cites the rate card or marks “confirm rate.”
5. Paste-ready contract or terms language for the common gaps (revisions, payment schedule, IP, cancellation).
6. A short clarification list of the three questions that still need an answer before signature.

This is **not** legal advice, **not** a full CRM, and **not** a guarantee the client will sign. It is a productized research-and-writing desk that turns the operator’s own materials into an inspectable commercial pack they can send today.

## Target user

- Primary buyer: EU/UK/US/Canada freelancers, indie hackers, and 1–3 person service shops (design, development, copy, marketing, ops) who do 2–8 discovery conversations a month.
- Urgent pain: the call went well, yet writing the proposal takes the rest of the evening and the client has already moved on by morning.
- Existing workaround: a half-finished Google Doc, a ChatGPT dump that still needs heavy editing, or delaying the send until the weekend.

## Why this works

- Market signal: this week’s posts treat “turn the notes into the proposal” as a ready-made workflow that solopreneurs already want. The missing artifact is a cited pack that stays faithful to what was actually said.
- Agent advantage: parse messy notes and threads; align every line of the SOW to a source quote; refuse to invent scope or rates that are not in the materials.
- Solo-operator advantage: one person who has written hundreds of proposals can tighten voice, catch the three real risks, and ship the pack in under 30 minutes of review.

## Monetization

- Primary model: per-pack service, plus a retainer for freelancers who want the desk on every new lead.
- First price test: 79 EUR/USD for a standard discovery-to-proposal pack; 129 if the materials include a long thread or multiple prior documents; 159 for same-day (4–8 h).
- Upsell: 39 “revision round” after client feedback; 149/month retainer for up to four packs; 29 “common terms library” pack of the most-used clauses.

3. Success bar: 4 serious replies and 2 paid packs in 14 days. Kill if 20 outbound touches produce zero deposits.

## Execution plan (one person + agents)

**Agent 1 — Intake.** Accept notes, transcripts, emails, rate cards, and prior SOWs. Normalize, assign source ids, flag missing detail.

**Agent 2 — Extractor.** Pull every stated requirement, constraint, budget mention, and timeline. Tag every fact with a source id or “inferred — confirm.”

**Agent 3 — Scope drafter.** Write the one-page scope matrix and the clarification list. Cite sources; refuse to invent deliverables.

**Agent 4 — Proposal writer.** Produce the proposal letter, numbered SOW, quote table, and paste-ready terms so the solo can drop them into their template.

**Human operator.** Remove invented scope or rates, tighten voice, confirm every claim is checkable against the materials, and export Markdown + PDF. Do not negotiate or send the proposal for the client.

Stack to start: existing coding/browser agents, one LLM, plain Markdown, Stripe Payment Link, mailbox. No product UI until the fifth paid job.

## Risks and mitigations

- Invented scope or rates: every claim cites the materials provided or is marked “inferred — confirm.” Operator rejects drafts that invent deliverables or prices.
- Confidential notes: isolated processing, no training on client work, delete after 30 days unless they opt in.
- Overlap with the 2026-11-13 pre-call brief: that pack prepares the solo for the call; this pack turns the call into the commercial document. Sell both; do not merge the files.
- Buyer expects the operator to negotiate or chase the client: deliver the pack and stop. Live negotiation is a separate engagement.
- Thin materials: if the customer has only a one-line “they want a website,” return a short gap list and partial refund rather than invent a full SOW.
