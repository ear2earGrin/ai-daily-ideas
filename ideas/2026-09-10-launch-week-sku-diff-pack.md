**Date:** September 10, 2026

Cheap agent reading + a 24-hour hardware trend spike = a productized SKU-diff desk, not another gadget blog.

## Concept

Sell a **24-hour launch-week SKU diff pack** for one product family the seller already makes.

The customer (indie case, dock, cable, controller, or skin seller; 1-person or 1-8 person shop) uploads:

- a SKU list: product name, target device, critical dimensions / ports / magnet layout / material notes, current stock, reprint lead time
- optional: last year's CAD notes, Amazon titles, or a photo of the current mold
- the launch they care about (this week: Apple / iPhone, Sony / PlayStation, Xbox) or a public keynote URL

Agents plus one human operator return within 24 hours of the official page landing:

1. A ranked SKU table (8-30 rows): SKU, current device, change (`fit / maybe / break / unknown`), the dimension or port that moved, confidence, recommended action (`keep selling / reprint / pause ads / kill`).
2. A rumor-vs-official split. X claims that are not on an official spec page or a named teardown are marked `unverified` and do not drive a kill decision.
3. Three ready-to-paste customer replies ("will this fit the new model?") plus two Amazon/Shopify title patches if a name or generation token changed.
4. A 14-day watchlist: what still needs a teardown photo before they spend on a new mold.
5. A source appendix. Every change cell cites an official URL, a timestamped screenshot, a teardown post, or `not-in-record`.

This is **not** a news site, **not** a CAD service, and **not** unauthorized resale of embargoed photos. It is a clerk that turns launch-day noise into a keep-or-kill list.

## Target user

- Primary buyer: EU/UK/US indie accessory sellers with 8-40 live SKUs who already lost a week of ads the last time a port or magnet layout moved and they found out from one-star reviews.
- Urgent pain: Apple / Sony / Xbox is trending, support inbox says "does this fit," and the official PDF is 40 pages of marketing.
- Existing workaround: watching YouTube teardowns at 1am, a Discord rumor channel, or reprinting everything "just in case."

## Why this works

- Market signal: today's worldwide X list is hardware platforms, not SaaS renewals. The catalog already covers vendor paper and intent replies. Nobody in the catalog owns launch-day physical compatibility.
- Agent advantage: pull official spec pages, compare last year's public dimensions, extract port and magnet language, refuse to treat a viral render as a measurement.
- Solo-operator advantage: one person who has shipped a physical accessory can tell a marketing "thinner" claim from a 0.4mm camera-bump change that breaks a mold. Judgment is the product; the model is the clerk.

## Monetization

- First price test: 149 EUR / 149 USD for one launch + up to 20 SKUs, 24-hour SLA after the official page is public; 249 for 21-40 SKUs; 79 add-on for a mid-week teardown refresh.

## Validation plan

1. Riskiest assumption: an accessory seller will pay ~149 for a cited fit table instead of watching teardowns and guessing.
2. Demand test: post one anonymized sample pack on X the same day a hardware trend spikes. DM 25 Etsy / Amazon / Shopify sellers who posted "does this fit the new iPhone / DualSense / Xbox." Offer the first 5 packs at 99 / 24h.
3. Success bar: 5 serious replies and 2 paid packs in 14 days, ideally inside one launch week. Kill if 25 outbound touches produce zero deposits.

## Execution plan (one person + agents)

**Agent 1 — Intake.** Normalize the SKU list. Extract device family, critical dimensions, ports, magnets, stock, lead time. Refuse to invent SKUs from a storefront scrape unless the owner pastes the export.

**Agent 2 — Official reader.** Load the manufacturer's spec page, support PDF, and developer accessory notes. Extract dimensions, ports, charging, magnet, camera-bump, and button language. Timestamp the capture.

**Agent 3 — Rumor filter.** Pull the day's high-engagement X posts and named teardown accounts. Tag each claim `official / teardown / rumor`. Never let a rumor flip a row to `kill`.

**Agent 4 — Diff engine.** Compare last-gen public numbers to this-gen official numbers for each SKU's critical field. Mark `unknown` when last-gen data is missing.

**Agent 5 — Pack drafter.** Fill the table, three customer replies, two title patches, and the 14-day watchlist. Every cell cites a source id or `not-in-record`. Refuse to place a factory order.

**Human operator.** Kill rows that overclaim a fit, drop embargoed images, export PDF + CSV. The model does not email the factory or change the Shopify catalog.

Stack to start: coding + browser agents, one LLM, a screenshot folder, Stripe Payment Link, a mailbox. No product UI until the fifth paid job. No scraping of private seller dashboards.

## First 7-day action plan

7. Keep or kill.

## Risks and mitigations

- Wrong kill decision: `kill` requires an official or named-teardown measurement. Rumor-only rows stay `unknown`.
- Embargo / stolen renders: do not include images that are not on an official page or a public teardown the owner could have seen.
- Liability for a bad fit: sell a research pack. The owner decides reprints. Do not call it a certified accessory program.
- Overlap with cited signal pack: if they want tweets about the launch, send them to 2026-09-05. This pack is their inventory list.
- Seasonal demand: if there is no launch this week, sell the teardown-refresh add-on against last week's pack instead of inventing a launch.
