## Concept

Sell a **same-week (or same-day) agent acquisition analyst pack** when a solopreneur or freelancer has run a cycle of agent-assisted outreach, content, or calls and needs to know what to double down on versus kill.

The customer pastes or uploads:

- recent outreach messages and replies (or export)
- call notes or recording transcripts (optional)
- simple metrics if available (opens, replies, booked calls, closed deals)
- the offers or angles tested
- optional: voice notes on what felt promising

Agents plus one human operator return within 24–72 hours:

1. A ranked table of offers/angles with reply rate, booking rate, or qualitative signal, each cited to source logs.
2. A clear kill list: what to stop immediately, with the evidence.
3. A keep-and-scale list with suggested volume or message tweaks.
4. Next-cycle prompt or instruction snippets the solo can paste into their Scout or Writer agents.
5. A short pattern note on what the data actually showed versus what felt true.
6. An appendix of source IDs so every ranking is checkable.

This is **not** full campaign management, **not** live sending, and **not** a guarantee of higher reply rates. It is a productized research-and-analysis desk that turns the week’s scattered agent outputs into an inspectable analyst pack the operator can act on the same week.

## Target user

- Primary buyer: EU/UK/US/Canada solopreneurs, indie hackers, and 1–3 person agencies already running agent stacks for client acquisition (outreach, content, lead gen) who skip the analysis step.
- Urgent pain: generation and sending are cheap and automated; deciding what actually worked still takes evenings of spreadsheet staring or gut feel. Most skip the Analyst agent entirely.
- Existing workaround: glance at reply counts, ask another model “what worked,” or keep running the same sequence because changing it feels risky.

## Why this works

- Market signal: this week’s posts treat the full Scout-to-Analyst stack as realistic for one person, yet explicitly flag that the Analyst step is the one people skip. Quiet failures and missing feedback loops are named as the real reason builders fail.
- Agent advantage: ingest messy logs and exports, align outcomes to specific messages or offers, refuse to invent conversion numbers not present in the data, and surface ranked patterns with citations.
- Solo-operator advantage: one person who has run similar acquisition cycles can drop weak rankings, tighten the kill-list language, and ship a pack in under 40 minutes of review. Domain judgment is the product; the model is the clerk.

## Execution plan (one person + agents)

**Agent 1 — Intake.** Accept logs, notes, and metrics. Normalize formats and flag missing detail or uncited numbers.

**Agent 2 — Outcome mapper.** Align replies, bookings, or qualitative signals to specific messages, offers, or angles. Cite every source; mark anything inferred.

**Agent 3 — Ranker and kill-list writer.** Produce the ranked table, keep list, and kill list. Refuse invented conversion rates.

**Agent 4 — Next-cycle drafter.** Write paste-ready prompt or instruction snippets based on the patterns. Cite the evidence.

**Human operator.** Remove invented facts, drop weak rankings, tighten kill-list language, and export Markdown + PDF. Do not run the next cycle or send messages on their behalf.

Stack to start: browser/search agents, one LLM, plain Markdown, Stripe Payment Link, mailbox. No product UI until the fifth paid job.

## Risks and mitigations

- Invented conversion rates or false rankings: only materials the customer supplies; every claim cites a source ID or is marked “inferred — confirm.” Operator rejects drafts that invent metrics.
- Over-claiming as growth strategy or performance guarantee: deliver a research pack and ranked lists only. Disclaimer on every PDF.
- Thin or messy input: if the customer supplies fewer than a handful of clear interactions, return a shorter pack and partial refund rather than invent patterns.
- Overlap with earlier radar or outreach packs: those produce the next messages or opportunities; this pack analyzes what already ran. Differentiate in sales copy.
- Buyer expects the operator to rewrite the entire sequence or run the next cycle: deliver the pack and stop. Live rewriting or campaign management is a separate engagement.
