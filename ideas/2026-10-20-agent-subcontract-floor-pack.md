# Agent Subcontract Scope-and-Receipt Floor Pack for Solo Operators

**Date:** October 20, 2026

## X signal (today)

X on 17–19 October 2026 is full of one-person companies and agent economies, but the practical gap is clear: agents that try to do everything, or open-ended hiring of other agents, quickly lose accountability.

- Posts describe one-person stacks where agents handle marketing, sales, support, research, and product while the human keeps strategy. The next step many are exploring is agents that *subcontract* (hire specialized agents for art, animation, code, research, or trading).
- Agent marketplaces and paid production seats (LAX-style, Clawpump, etc.) are appearing, with wallets, risk limits, and the question of how to settle quality and disputes.
- Adjacent threads note that without clear scopes, receipts, and evaluation rules, multi-agent work is just APIs talking to each other. Operators want autonomy without discovering the hard way that permission ≠ good judgment.

The catalog has spend-boundary, access-policy, exception-review, and identity packs. It does not yet have a productized desk specifically for *subcontracting* specialized agents with a tight scope + receipt.

## Concept

Sell an **agent subcontract scope-and-receipt floor pack** when a solopreneur, indie hacker, or small studio wants to hand a discrete job to one or more specialized agents (or an agent marketplace) and needs guardrails before money or reputation moves.

The customer uploads or pastes:

- the job brief (what they want done, by when, any existing materials)
- preferred agent types, tools, or marketplace listings (or a description of the specialist they want)
- budget ceiling and any hard constraints (no external calls, no spend above X, human approval for Y)
- optional: prior agent logs or a sample of acceptable output

Agents plus one human operator return within 48 hours:

1. A tight job spec: inputs, expected outputs, success criteria, and an explicit “do-not-do” list.
2. A boundary card: spend ceiling, approval gates, off-switch rules, and data-handling notes. Every limit cites the customer’s material or is marked “draft-unverified.”
3. An evaluation checklist and sample receipt template (what was delivered, citations to the brief, open questions, pass/fail signals).
4. A short handoff note the human (or another agent) can use to start the job and later review the result.
5. A source appendix. No invented marketplace fees or capabilities.

This is **not** actually hiring the agents, **not** running the job, and **not** a full agent orchestration platform. It is a productized desk that turns “I want an agent to do X” into an inspectable floor the solo can execute safely.

## Target user

- Primary buyer: solopreneurs, indie hackers, and freelance operators (EU/UK/US) who are experimenting with multi-agent setups or agent marketplaces and have already felt the pain of vague scopes or surprise spend.
- Urgent pain: they want the leverage of specialized agents but currently either do the work themselves or give overly broad access and then spend time cleaning up.
- Existing workaround: write a loose prompt, watch the run, or avoid subcontracting altogether.

## Why this works

- Market signal: today’s feed treats one-person agent teams and agent-to-agent hiring as the next practical step, while also highlighting the missing accountability layer (scopes, receipts, limits).
- Agent advantage: structure a messy brief into a clear spec, extract constraints, and produce consistent checklists and templates overnight.
- Solo-operator advantage: one person who has run agent jobs can tighten the “do-not-do” list, confirm realism, and deliver a pack the buyer can act on in under 40 minutes of review.

## Execution plan (one person + agents)

**Agent 1 — Intake.** Accept brief, constraints, and any listings. Normalize and assign a job id. Flag missing budget or success criteria.

**Agent 2 — Spec writer.** Produce the tight job spec and “do-not-do” list, citing only provided material.

**Agent 3 — Boundary agent.** Draft spend, approval, and off-switch rules. Mark anything not in the customer materials as draft-unverified.

**Agent 4 — Receipt + handoff agent.** Build the evaluation checklist, receipt template, and short handoff note.

**Human operator.** Cut unrealistic capabilities, confirm no invented fees, and export Markdown + PDF. Do not hire agents or move money.

Stack to start: existing coding/browser agents, one LLM, plain Markdown or python-docx, Stripe Payment Link, mailbox. No product UI until the fifth paid job.

## Risks and mitigations

- Invented capabilities or fees: every limit or claim cites the customer’s material or is marked draft-unverified. Operator rejects drafts that add marketplace features not provided.
- Scope creep into actually running the agents: deliver the floor and stop. Execution is a separate engagement.
- Overlap with spend-boundary or access-policy packs: those cover internal agent permissions and budgets. This pack is specifically the *external subcontract* spec + receipt for hiring other agents. Sell both when relevant; do not merge files.
- Buyer expects guaranteed quality from the hired agent: the pack sets evaluation criteria; it does not guarantee the external agent’s output.
- Low-quality brief: if the input is too vague, return a short note and partial refund rather than invent a scope.
