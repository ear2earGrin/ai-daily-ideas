# Same-Day Odd-Job Proof Listing Pack for Agent Marketplaces

**Date:** October 1, 2026

## X signal (today)

X on 1 October 2026 is showing what an agent marketplace looks like when it stops being a demo: tiny, oddly specific jobs with a price, a deliverable, and a track record.

- Merida (@nafas22000, 1 Oct): on TermiX / agent.family you can pay an agent $1 to turn an idea into a custom 3D holographic collectible, or pay another to be Player 2 in a classic NES game. The claim is that the agent economy is thousands of one-thing businesses, not one "AI services" category. Quoted workflow: agents learn procedures (register, stake, create jobs, make offers, submit deliverables, check disputes) as installable skills instead of a human rewiring the protocol each time.
- Captain (@Captainmetax, 1 Oct): TermiX marketplace loop is post job → quote → escrow → deliver → challenge → on-chain settlement and reputation. A video-production agent is the example: brand, message, vibe, logo in; prompt → visuals → music → final cut out.
- Omai Leidi (@nokaramo, 1 Oct): Search is for services, not bot names. Filters are budget, delivery time, reputation, verification tier, pass rate, and completed jobs. Categories include Code, Security, Data, Design, Writing, Automation, Research, Model Ops. Loop named: DISCOVER → BID → EXECUTE → VERIFY → SETTLE.
- Adjacent, not today's post but the same shape: Greg Isenberg's tiny watching-agents (domain drops, hiring signals, sunset SaaS) and Superteam AU bounties paying $500 to build and demo an agent skill. The catalog already has a SKILL.md hire surface (2026-09-30) and an agent-hire listing pack (2026-09-06). It does not have a same-day *proof job*: a fixed $1–$20 deliverable with acceptance checks, built so the first completed jobs exist before anyone hires the expensive version.

## Concept

Sell a **same-day odd-job proof listing pack** to a solo who already has one narrow agent skill and zero marketplace reputation.

The customer sends:

- the one thing the agent already does well (a replay clip, a one-page brief, a cited diff, a collectible render)
- what "done" looks like, in one sentence
- what it must never do (spend money, post publicly, touch production logins)
- a price they would actually accept for the proof version ($1, $5, or $20)
- which marketplace they can already reach (agent.family, a bounty board, their own site, or just a public X offer)

Agents plus one human operator return:

1. A proof-job card: title, price, delivery time, inputs required, output format, what is out of scope.
2. An acceptance checklist a buyer or another agent can run without chatting (file exists, length, citation, no secrets).
3. A sample deliverable from public or synthetic inputs, so the listing is not an empty promise.
4. A listing blurb in marketplace voice and a shorter X post.
5. A reputation seed note: which three completed proof jobs to do before raising the price, and what the paid upsell is.
6. A source appendix. No claim the pack registered the agent on-chain unless the owner clicks.

This is **not** a marketplace, **not** a token, and **not** a general "list your agent" generator. It is one weird job, priced, with a receipt shape.

Fun variant if nobody pays this week: ship the proof job yourself. An agent that finishes one NES level as Player 2, or turns a one-sentence brief into a holographic-style collectible card, is a demo and a listing at the same time.

## Target user

- Primary buyer: solo agent builders and freelancers who can already produce one narrow deliverable and are staring at a marketplace search page with zero completed jobs.
- Urgent pain: search filters reward pass rate and completed jobs. A new agent with a clever skill is invisible until someone has paid it and accepted the work.
- Existing workaround: posting a demo thread and hoping, or underpricing the real service on day one.

## Why this works

- Market signal: today's posts are literally "$1 holographic collectible" and "Player 2 in an NES game," plus search by budget, delivery time, and completed jobs. Reputation is the filter. A proof job is the cheapest way to get a row in that filter.
- Agent advantage: draft the card, the checklist, and the sample from a real output the owner already has. Refuse to invent a pass rate.
- Solo-operator advantage: one person can finish three $5 jobs in an afternoon if the scope is frozen. Judgment is which odd job is specific enough to be bought and narrow enough to not become a support desk.

## Monetization

- Primary model: per-pack service, plus optional "I run the first three proof jobs for you."
- First price test: 79 USD / 79 EUR for one skill, one listing card, one sample, one checklist. 149 if the operator also submits the listing and delivers the first proof job.
- Upsell: 39 per extra marketplace rewrite; 99/month to refresh the card when the skill changes; the owner's own proof jobs at $1–$20 are the public funnel into a $150 real engagement.
- Fun / public variant: publish one synthetic pack (NES Player 2 clip, or a collectible card from a public-domain brief). Never include wallet keys or client files.

## Validation plan

1. Riskiest assumption: a solo will pay ~79 for a listing card instead of writing it themselves in twenty minutes.
2. Demand test: publish one public proof listing (a $5 "one-page cited reply to a public brief" or a fun collectible card) on 1–3 Oct. DM 20 people posting about agent marketplaces, zero completed jobs, or skill bounties. Offer the first 5 packs at 49.
3. Success bar: 5 serious replies and 2 paid packs, or 3 strangers paying for the public proof job, in 14 days. Kill if 20 outbound touches and a live $5 offer produce zero deposits.

## Execution plan (one person + agents)

**Agent 1 — Intake.** Accept the skill description, a sample output, price, and never-do list. Refuse wallet seeds, API keys, and production cookies.

**Agent 2 — Scope freezer.** Cut the skill to one input, one output, one time box. Mark anything that needs a human click as `owner-step`.

**Agent 3 — Checklist writer.** Acceptance checks a buyer can run on the file alone. Every check cites the sample or the owner's rule.

**Agent 4 — Pack writer.** Card, checklist, sample, blurb, X post, reputation-seed note, appendix.

**Human operator.** Kill invented reputation stats, strip secrets, export Markdown + PDF. The model does not register, stake, or settle on-chain.

Stack to start: one LLM, a Stripe Payment Link or a plain crypto invoice the owner already uses, a mailbox. No new chain integration in week one.

## First 7-day action plan

1. Pick one odd job you can finish in under 30 minutes (cited one-pager, or a fun collectible / replay clip).
2. Write the default card: price, inputs, output, out of scope, 24-hour delivery.
3. Publish the sample listing and the price on a one-pager and X.
4. Send 20 outbound notes to people asking how agents get hired or showing empty reputation.
5. Deliver two paid proof jobs yourself so the listing has receipts.
6. Time human review. Target under 25 minutes after job two.
7. Keep or kill.

## Risks and mitigations

- Marketplace vapor: do not promise on-chain escrow, staking, or dispute resolution. Link the owner's actual venue or fall back to a public invoice plus delivery.
- Overlap with SKILL.md hire surface (2026-09-30): that pack makes a skill installable by other agents. This pack makes one job buyable and acceptable by a stranger today.
- Overlap with agent-hire listing (2026-09-06): that pack is a general hire profile. This pack is a single priced proof job whose only purpose is the first completed-job row.
- Race-to-the-bottom pricing: the $1 job is a receipt, not the business. The pack must name the upsell or it trains buyers to expect the real work for a dollar.
- IP and games: a Player 2 demo stays on public-domain or owner-owned ROMs. Do not ship copyrighted game assets.
- Secrets in sample inputs: halt and delete if keys appear.
