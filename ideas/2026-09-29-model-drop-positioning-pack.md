# Same-Week Model-Drop Positioning Pack for Indie AI Sellers

**Date:** September 29, 2026

## X signal (today)

X on 28-29 September 2026 is treating **model drops as a marketing event, not a changelog.**

- Worldwide trends on 29 Sep include **DevDay**, **Sonnet 5.5**, **World Labs**, and earlier **GPT-6.1 Astra**. The conversation is not "is the model good" so much as "what do I change on my site tonight."
- Greg Isenberg's ongoing frame (info arbitrage + agents show up with the math already done) is the same motion: harvest a public release, build the buyer-facing packet before the seller has time to write it.
- Alex Nguyen's evergreen list still holds: people pay for studying, productivity, money, and product tools. A model drop is just a weekly reason to refresh those pages.
- Sambam's "boring jobs businesses already need, made faster with AI" applies: indie sellers already rewrite comparison tables by hand every time Anthropic, OpenAI, Google, or a lab ships. They just do it late and sloppy.
- Adjacent catalog packs already cover persona landing evals (2026-09-27), launch-week SKU diffs for hardware accessories (2026-09-10), and ship-receipt distribution floors (2026-09-23). They do not own a *same-week model-drop positioning pack*: one product, one new model, one cited diff of what actually changed for the buyer.

## Concept

Sell a **same-week model-drop positioning pack** when an indie SaaS, wrapper, agent shop, or API product needs to answer "so what does Sonnet 5.5 / DevDay / World Labs change for *my* buyer" before the weekend.

The customer uploads or points at:

- current landing page, pricing page, and one comparison table (URLs or Markdown)
- the model or platform drop they care about (name + official notes URL)
- last 7 days of support tickets or X replies asking "does this work with X yet"
- optional: their eval set or a 10-row before/after sample

Agents plus one human operator return within 48 hours:

1. A drop floor: claims tagged `verified-in-notes` / `vendor-marketing` / `untested-on-your-product` / `do-not-say`.
2. A one-page offer diff: old headline vs new headline, three bullets that a buyer can check, one sentence that must not be said until they run their own eval.
3. A comparison-table patch: only cells that cite the vendor notes or the customer's own sample. Blank cells stay blank.
4. A 7-line X + email script: announce the drop, link the eval caveat, ask for one screenshot from a paying user.
5. A first-week script: run the 10-row sample tonight; pin the `do-not-say` list in the agent that writes social; do not auto-push the live site.
6. A source appendix. No claim that their product "is now state of the art."

This is **not** a general landing-page agency, **not** an eval platform, and **not** a fake benchmark farm. It is a productized desk for *one seller, one drop, one week*.

## Target user

- Primary buyer: EU/US indie AI tool makers, wrapper shops, and 1-3 person agent consultancies with a live product page.
- Urgent pain: a model drops Tuesday; by Thursday their page still says last quarter's model name and Discord is asking if they support it.
- Existing workaround: paste the release blog into ChatGPT, rewrite three headlines, ship, then walk back a claim on Friday.

## Why this works

- Market signal: today's trend list put DevDay and Sonnet 5.5 next to sports and coffee. Indie sellers treat those names as GTM events. The catalog already evaluates personas and ships launch receipts. It does not freeze *this week's model name* into a cited offer diff.
- Agent advantage: extract only claims that appear in official notes or the customer's sample; refuse invented latency and price numbers.
- Solo-operator advantage: one person who has shipped a tool can kill a hype line in two minutes. Judgment about `do-not-say` is the product; the model is the clerk.

## Monetization

- Primary model: per-pack service.
- First price test: 149 EUR / 149 USD for one product, one drop, 48h; 229 if they want the comparison table plus a 10-row eval template filled from their sample.
- Upsell: 49 rush (24h); 79 mid-week rewrite if the vendor posts a follow-up system card; 329/month retainer (every public model drop that touches their stack); 39 add-on Notion poster of the `do-not-say` list.
- Fun / public variant: publish one synthetic pack ("notes say long context; seller has no 100k eval; headline must not say 'handles whole codebases'"). Never publish a customer's unpublished evals as if they were official scores.

## Validation plan

1. Riskiest assumption: an indie seller will pay ~149 for a cited offer diff instead of prompting ChatGPT with the release blog.
2. Demand test: post one anonymized sample pack on X on 29 Sep-1 Oct. DM 25 people who posted "updated for Sonnet 5.5", "DevDay recap", or "does this work with the new model." Offer the first 5 packs at 99 / 48h SLA.
3. Success bar: 5 serious replies and 2 paid packs in 14 days. Kill if 25 outbound touches produce zero deposits.

## Execution plan (one person + agents)

**Agent 1 — Intake.** Accept URLs, notes links, tickets, sample rows. Detect one product and one drop. Refuse unpublished vendor leaks presented as facts.

**Agent 2 — Notes miner.** Pull official posts and system cards. Tag each claim. Separate vendor marketing from measurable limits.

**Agent 3 — Page diff.** Compare current copy to the drop. Flag stale model names, invented benchmarks, and missing caveats.

**Agent 4 — Pack writer.** Floor, offer diff, table patch, 7-line script, first-week script. Every number cites notes, the sample, or `draft-unverified`.

**Human operator.** Kill hype, strip private eval numbers the customer did not clear, export PDF + Markdown. The model does not push the live site.

Stack to start: coding + browse agents, one LLM, Stripe Payment Link, a mailbox. Public notes + customer URLs only in week one.

## First 7-day action plan

1. Write a default claim standard (`verified-in-notes` vs `untested-on-your-product` vs `do-not-say`).
2. Build one public sample pack from a synthetic writing-tool + Sonnet 5.5 notes pair.
3. Publish sample + prices + "we do not invent benchmarks" disclaimer on a one-pager and X.
4. Send 25 outbound notes to model-drop and "does it work with X" posts.
5. Run two paid pilots.
6. Time human review. Target under 40 minutes after job two.
7. Keep or kill.

## Risks and mitigations

- Invented benchmarks: never fill a latency, price, or quality cell without a citation. Blank is allowed.
- Overlap with persona landing eval (2026-09-27): that pack tests personas against a page. This pack patches the page for one named model drop.
- Overlap with launch-week SKU diff (2026-09-10): that pack is hardware accessories. This pack is software positioning after a lab release.
- Overlap with ship-receipt distribution (2026-09-23): that pack is how you announce a ship. This pack is what you are allowed to claim about a third-party model.
- Customer wants you to "just update Webflow": week one delivers files. They push, or they pay a separate 79 apply-the-diff add-on with a human click.
