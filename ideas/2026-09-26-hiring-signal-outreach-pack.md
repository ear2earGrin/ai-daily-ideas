## Concept

Sell a **same-week hiring-signal outreach pack** when a solo agency, freelancer, or vertical SaaS seller already has an offer and is guessing who is in-market.

The customer sends:

- one sentence of what they sell and who they sell it to
- 1–3 example closed deals or case studies (links or PDFs)
- optional: the boards they already watch (LinkedIn, Indeed, Greenhouse pages, X lists)
- optional: a "do not contact" list

Agents plus one human operator return within 48 hours:

1. A signal map: 8–15 live job posts or career-page changes from the last 7 days that match the offer. Each row cites URL, posted date, role family, and *why it is a buy signal* in one line.
2. A fit scorecard: `strong` / `maybe` / `noise`. No invented headcount or budget.
3. Five draft outreach notes (email or X DM) grounded in the cited post. No "I noticed you are growing."
4. A 7-day watch list: which company pages to re-check next week and what change would upgrade `maybe` to `strong`.
5. A source appendix. If a date or posting is gone, mark `not-in-record`.

This is **not** a scraped lead list, **not** a sequency SaaS, and **not** an auto-sender. It is one week's evidence plus five drafts the human actually sends.

## Target user

- Primary buyer: EU/UK/US solo agencies and B2B freelancers selling to shops that hire (marketing, ops, bookkeeping, web, AI implementation).
- Urgent pain: they know hiring = budget, but they spend Sunday night doom-scrolling LinkedIn and then send generic DMs on Monday.
- Existing workaround: Sales Navigator alerts they ignore, a VA who pastes titles into a sheet, or an agent that invents "they just raised."

## Why this works

- Market signal: this week's X feed named hiring posts as buying signals and morning draft-outreach loops. The catalog's intent-reply pack watches complaints; this pack watches *headcount*.
- Agent advantage: fetch public postings, refuse missing dates, draft notes that quote the role instead of flattering the brand.
- Solo-operator advantage: one person who has sold into a hiring company can kill the `maybe` rows in ten minutes. Taste is the product; the model is the clerk.

## Execution plan (one person + agents)

**Agent 1 — Intake.** Lock one offer and one buyer type. Refuse "anyone who hires."

**Agent 2 — Board watcher.** Pull public job URLs from the last 7 days only. Tag each `live-url` / `cached-gone` / `not-in-record`.

**Agent 3 — Fit clerk.** Score against the offer and closed-deal examples. Drop recruiter spam and intern posts unless the customer sells intern programs.

**Agent 4 — Draft writer.** Five notes. Every claim cites a posting line. No invented funding rounds.

**Human operator.** Kill creepy personalization, drop private profile scraping, export PDF + Markdown. The model does not send the email.

Stack to start: coding + browser agents, one LLM, Stripe Payment Link, a mailbox. Public pages only in week one.

## Risks and mitigations

- Scraping private LinkedIn at scale: week one is public career pages and public job URLs the customer can open without login.
- Overlap with problem-intent replies (2026-09-09): that pack answers people describing a problem. This pack starts from a *hire*. Sell both; do not merge the files.
- Overlap with cited signal pack (2026-09-05): that pack is content/distribution signals. This pack is headcount signals.
- Auto-sending: never connect Gmail. Drafts only.
- Scope creep into a lead-gen SaaS: deliver the week. Stop.
