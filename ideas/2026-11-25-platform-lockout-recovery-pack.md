## Concept

Sell a **same-week platform lockout recovery pack** when a solopreneur or 1–3 person shop wants agent leverage on travel, banking, messaging, or similar restricted surfaces and has just hit (or expects) a block.

The customer pastes or uploads:

- list of platforms and the exact actions they wanted the agent to do
- current manual workaround (screenshots, notes, or “I just do it myself”)
- risk tolerance and any existing human approval steps
- optional: public terms or recent error messages

Agents plus one human operator return within 24–72 hours:

1. A lockout map: each action tagged `blocked`, `human-session-ok`, `read-only-monitor`, or `open` with a cited source.
2. Credential-free alternatives: browser handoff scripts, queued approval cards, local-only notes, or “do this once and let the agent watch the confirmation.”
3. A short human-approval checklist the owner can run in under two minutes.
4. Paste-ready system prompts or wrapper snippets that refuse credential requests and surface “needs human” instead.
5. A one-page “what changed this week” note so the floor stays current.
6. Source appendix. No “just use a residential proxy” or credential-sharing advice.

This is **not** a bypass service, **not** account access, and **not** legal advice. It is a productized compliance desk that keeps the human in control and the agent useful.

## Target user

- Primary buyer: EU/UK/US solopreneurs, indie hackers, and small shops already running personal or client agents who just got blocked on United, a bank, or a messaging app.
- Urgent pain: the agent that was supposed to book the flight or check the balance now fails; the human is back to doing it manually and the productivity gain vanished.
- Existing workaround: share passwords (risky), abandon the agent for that task, or wait for official APIs that may never come.

## Why this works

- Market signal: this week’s posts treat platform lockouts as an immediate, concrete blocker to the agent future. Existing catalog packs assume the agent can reach the tool; this one starts from the refusal.
- Agent advantage: parse terms and news, map allowed vs blocked surfaces, draft refusal-aware prompts, and produce consistent cited alternatives without inventing workarounds.
- Solo-operator advantage: one person who has watched an agent get blocked can write the two-minute approval card and kill any suggestion that smells like credential sharing. Judgment about acceptable risk is the product; the model is the clerk.

## Execution plan (one person + agents)

**Agent 1 — Intake.** Accept platform list + desired actions. Flag anything that requires credentials and refuse it.

**Agent 2 — Policy & news parser.** Pull public terms and recent reporting. Tag each action. Cite the source URL or mark `not-in-record`.

**Agent 3 — Recovery designer.** Draft human-handoff scripts, approval cards, and refusal-aware prompts. No proxies, no credential vaults that leave the user’s machine.

**Agent 4 — Pack writer.** Lockout map, checklists, snippets, and appendix. Every claim cites a source or is marked “inferred — confirm.”

**Human operator.** Kill any borderline suggestion, tighten language, export Markdown + PDF. Do not log into any customer account.

Stack to start: browser/search agents, one LLM, Stripe Payment Link, mailbox. Exports only.

## Risks and mitigations

- Accidental bypass advice: operator rejects anything that suggests proxies, credential sharing, or terms violation. Pack is recovery + human floor only.
- Overlap with review or overnight packs: those assume the agent can reach the tool. This pack starts from the lockout. Differentiate clearly.
- Stale terms: every pack includes the date of the sources and a note that platforms can change rules overnight.
- Buyer expects the operator to “make the agent work”: deliver the floor and stop. Live integration is a separate engagement.
