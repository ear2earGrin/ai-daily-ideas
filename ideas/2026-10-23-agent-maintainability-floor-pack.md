## Concept

Sell a **same-week agent maintainability floor pack** when a solopreneur, indie hacker, or freelance operator has agents producing code, docs, workflows, or content that ships fast but becomes opaque and risky to change.

The customer uploads (or pastes):

- 2–4 recent real agent-generated artifacts (code files, workflow exports, content drafts, research notes)
- the intended job of each agent and what “ownable” looks like
- optional: current pain notes (“I can’t explain this file” or “I’m afraid to touch it”)

Agents plus one human operator return within 48–72 hours:

1. One-sentence ownership template: every future artifact must open with a single sentence that a human can read and decide whether to ship or escalate.
2. Required receipts & citation rules: what must be present (source ids, test pointers, change rationale, or “not in record”) for the artifact to be maintainable in under 60 seconds.
3. Escalation / off-switch language: short rules for when the human must intervene or when the agent must refuse to proceed.
4. Maintainability checklist + starter prompt changes: concrete edits so future runs emit the one-sentence note and receipts automatically.
5. Source ledger: every rule cites the customer’s sample artifacts or is marked “not specified — operator assumption.”

This is **not** a full code review, **not** ongoing monitoring, and **not** rewriting the agents. It is a productized desk that turns opaque agent output into an inspectable ownership floor the solo can hand back the same week.

## Target user

- Primary buyer: solopreneurs, indie hackers, and freelance operators (EU/UK/US) who already run 1–4 agents (Claude Code, Cursor, n8n, custom bots) and find that the artifacts ship faster than they can own them.
- Urgent pain: agent drafts are faster than trust or future understanding; the human either re-reads everything, ships opaque work, or stops using the agent.
- Existing workaround: one giant chat, ad-hoc “looks good,” manual rewrites, or turning the agent off.

## Why this works

- Market signal: today’s posts treat “if I can’t explain it in one sentence it doesn’t ship” and “human-in-the-loop as the cost of accountability” as the missing layer that makes agents stick beyond the first week. The missing artifact is a cited pack that forces self-documenting ownership.
- Agent advantage: extract opacity from samples, draft the one-sentence template and receipt rules, and produce ready-to-paste prompt changes overnight.
- Solo-operator advantage: one person who has watched ownership time erase generation gains can kill vague artifacts, tighten the floor, and deliver a pack the buyer can implement in under 40 minutes of review.

## Execution plan (one person + agents)

**Agent 1 — Intake.** Accept sample artifacts and notes. Normalize, assign ids, flag missing ownership criteria or sources.

**Agent 2 — Opacity mapper.** Extract what the human currently cannot explain or safely change. Flag gaps as “not specified.”

**Agent 3 — Floor drafter.** Write the one-sentence template, required receipts, citation rules, and escalation language. Cite sources.

**Agent 4 — Checklist writer.** Produce the maintainability checklist and starter prompt changes that force the floor on future runs.

**Human operator.** Remove vague rules, tighten the one-sentence test, confirm the floor is realistic, and export Markdown + PDF. Do not rewrite the live agents.

Stack to start: existing coding/browser agents, one LLM, plain Markdown, Stripe Payment Link, mailbox. No product UI until the fifth paid job.

## Risks and mitigations

- Invented maintainability rules: every field cites provided samples or is marked “not specified — operator assumption.” Operator rejects drafts that invent processes.
- Confidential artifacts: isolated processing, no training on client samples, delete after 30 days unless they opt in.
- Overlap with reviewability or ownership packs: this pack is specifically the long-term ownership floor and one-sentence self-documentation, not short review cards or ownership matrices. Implementation stays with the buyer.
- Buyer expects full prompt rewrite or monitoring: deliver the pack and stop. Connecting tools or running the agents is a separate engagement.
- Low-quality samples: if artifacts are too thin, return a short gap list and partial refund rather than invent rules.
