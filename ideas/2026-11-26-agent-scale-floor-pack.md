## Concept

Sell a **same-week (or same-day) agent scale floor pack** when a solopreneur or freelancer is hitting the limit of managing agents across more than one or two clients.

The customer pastes or uploads:

- a short description of the current agent stack and tools (Scout, Writer, Outreach, Closer, or whatever they run)
- client workflows or job types they already handle
- recent examples of handoffs, approvals, or near-misses (optional logs or notes)
- risk notes (“never send pricing,” “always check invoice amounts,” “client X is high-stakes”)

Agents plus one human operator return within 24–72 hours:

1. A risk-tier table (auto / low / medium / high) with clear criteria for each agent step, cited to the supplied notes or marked “inferred — confirm.”
2. Approval and exception rules: what the human must see, what can auto-proceed, and where an exception queue is required.
3. Silent-failure and missing-task alerts: concrete checks for “agent said done but nothing landed,” duplicate actions, or dropped handoffs.
4. Paste-ready config, wrapper, or instruction snippets the solo can drop into their existing stack.
5. A one-page operating model: who reviews what, expected review minutes per client, and the kill criteria for adding a new client.
6. An appendix of source IDs so every rule is checkable.

This is **not** full agent management, **not** live monitoring, and **not** a guarantee of zero errors. It is a productized research-and-architecture desk that turns the operator’s own stack into an inspectable scale floor they can use the same week.

## Target user

- Primary buyer: EU/UK/US/Canada solopreneurs, indie hackers, and 1–3 person agencies already running agent stacks who have 1–2 clients and want 3–5 more without hiring a reviewer.
- Urgent pain: generation and execution are cheap; managing quality, approvals, and handoffs still requires a second or third person once volume rises. Architecture was never set up for scale.
- Existing workaround: personally review every output, add more agents and hope, or delay taking the next client.

## Why this works

- Market signal: this week’s posts treat the “solofounder scaling wall” and silent handoff failures as the current bottleneck. Approval-first controls and human-yes rules are the practical next step once generation works.
- Agent advantage: ingest messy stack notes and near-miss examples, map them to concrete risk tiers and exception rules, refuse to invent failure modes not present in the materials, and surface paste-ready checks.
- Solo-operator advantage: one person who has scaled similar stacks can drop weak rules, tighten the operating model, and ship a pack in under 45 minutes of review. Domain judgment is the product; the model is the clerk.

## Execution plan (one person + agents)

**Agent 1 — Intake.** Accept stack notes, workflows, and risk examples. Normalize formats and flag missing detail.

**Agent 2 — Risk and handoff mapper.** Align steps to risk tiers and identify likely silent-failure points. Cite every source; mark anything inferred.

**Agent 3 — Rule and alert writer.** Produce the approval rules, exception queues, and concrete alert checks. Refuse invented failure modes.

**Agent 4 — Config and model drafter.** Write paste-ready snippets and the one-page operating model based on the rules. Cite the evidence.

**Human operator.** Remove invented facts, drop weak rules, tighten operating-model language, and export Markdown + PDF. Do not run or monitor their stack on their behalf.

Stack to start: browser/search agents, one LLM, plain Markdown, Stripe Payment Link, mailbox. No product UI until the fifth paid job.

## Risks and mitigations

- Invented rules or false failure modes: only materials the customer supplies; every claim cites a source ID or is marked “inferred — confirm.” Operator rejects drafts that invent stack details.
- Over-claiming as full ops management or zero-error guarantee: deliver a research pack and architecture only. Disclaimer on every PDF.
- Thin or messy input: if the customer supplies fewer than a handful of clear steps or notes, return a shorter pack and partial refund rather than invent architecture.
- Overlap with earlier review-floor or approval-lane packs: those handle a batch of drafts or a single approval path; this pack is the multi-client operating model and silent-failure floor. Differentiate in sales copy.
- Buyer expects the operator to implement or monitor the stack: deliver the pack and stop. Live implementation or monitoring is a separate engagement.
