# Same-Week Competitive Intel Brief for Solo Founders

**Date:** October 28, 2026

## X signal (today)

X and related discussion in late October 2026 keeps returning to the same solo-founder gap: agents are everywhere, but competitive awareness is still manual and surprising.

- Greg Isenberg-style threads and recaps continue to list "the competitive intel agent" among the most obvious tiny agent businesses: monitor a handful of rivals (pricing, pages, jobs, tweets) and deliver a one-page brief. The framework is "any job where someone spends hours checking for updates is an agent."
- Multiple posts and tools around agentic competitive intel (one-prompt or always-on scouting into the founder's existing agent workflow) are being shared and upvoted.
- Broader agent conversation emphasizes reliability, citations, and human approval for anything that could mislead. Solo founders repeatedly describe being surprised by a pricing change or feature that had been public for weeks.
- Adjacent: one-person AI workforce posts (research → outreach → build → support) still treat competitive watching as something the human does or ignores.

Existing catalog packs cover overnight ops floors, spend attribution, approval lanes, and exception control. They do not own a same-week, cited competitive brief scoped to 3–5 named rivals for a solo founder.

## Concept

Sell a **same-week competitive intel brief** when a solo founder, indie hacker, or 1–5 person shop wants to know what 3–5 competitors actually changed without spending Sunday mornings on tabs.

The customer provides:

- 3–5 competitor names + website URLs
- optional: pricing page, changelog, careers page, X/LinkedIn handles
- optional: one prior surprise ("they dropped price and I only heard from a prospect")

Agents plus one human operator return within 48 hours (or on a weekly cadence):

1. A change log limited to observable public signals: pricing, homepage/messaging, new features or changelog entries, job posts, notable social activity, recurring low-star review themes.
2. A citation appendix — every claim links to a source or is marked `not-in-record`.
3. A one-page brief: what moved, why it might matter, and **one** prioritized recommended action (or "no action this week").
4. Optional snapshot comparison for the single highest-signal change.

This is **not** a hosted always-on monitoring platform, **not** real-time coverage of 50 sources, and **not** an agent that logs into the customer's tools or acts on their behalf. It is a productized desk for *one founder, one set of rivals, one week*.

## Target user

- Primary buyer: EU/UK/US solo SaaS, info-product, or agency founders who already have customers and feel the pain of competitive surprises.
- Urgent pain: they know they should watch pricing, messaging, and feature moves but only do it sporadically; the information is public yet they still get blindsided.
- Existing workaround: Google Alerts that go unread, occasional manual checks, or enterprise CI tools they never open.

## Why this works

- Market signal: repeated X and indie-hacker discussion of tiny competitive-intel agents, one-prompt intel, and founders discovering pricing changes from prospects rather than their own monitoring.
- Agent advantage: browser agents, X search, and review scraping can gather the raw signals faster than a human; the model is good at drafting a scannable brief when forced to cite.
- Solo-operator advantage: one person who has been surprised by a competitor can judge which signals matter and kill weak claims. The product is the judgment + citations, not the raw scrape.

## Monetization

- Primary model: per-brief service and lightweight retainers.
- First price test: 99–149 EUR/USD for one set of 3–5 competitors (48h SLA). 79–129/month for a Monday brief.
- Upsell: extra competitors, deeper review-theme dig, or a one-page "what to ship or say next" action pack. Rush fee for 24h.
- Fun / public variant: publish one synthetic or fully anonymized sample brief. Never publish a real customer's competitor list without permission.

## Validation plan

1. Riskiest assumption: a solo founder will pay ~99–149 for a cited one-week brief instead of continuing to check manually or ignore the problem.
2. Demand test: post one public sample brief on X and in 2–3 indie-hacker / solo-founder spaces. DM 20–25 people who recently posted about competitor surprises, pricing changes, or "I should monitor this." Offer the first 5 briefs at a discount with a 48h SLA.
3. Success bar: 5 serious replies and 2 paid briefs in 14 days. Kill if 25 outbound touches produce zero deposits.

## Execution plan (one person + agents)

**Agent 1 — Intake.** Accept the list. Cap at 5 competitors. Refuse open-ended "monitor my whole market."

**Agent 2 — Browser scout.** Visit pricing, homepage, changelog, and careers pages. Note observable changes or capture current state. Never log into anything.

**Agent 3 — Social & review scout.** Pull recent public posts and scan public review/complaint sources for recurring themes.

**Agent 4 — Brief writer.** Produce the change log, citation appendix, one-page brief, and single recommended action. Every claim cites a source or is marked unverified.

**Human operator.** Kill invented numbers or missing links, ensure the recommended action is grounded, export clean Markdown/PDF, and deliver. The model does not contact competitors or act on the founder's accounts.

Stack to start: browser-capable agents, X/web search tools, one strong LLM, Stripe Payment Link, and a mailbox.

## First 7-day action plan

2. Build one public sample brief from 3 well-known indie tools.
3. Publish sample + prices + "citations only, no account access" disclaimer.
4. Send 20–25 outbound notes to posts about competitor surprises or monitoring pain.
5. Run two paid pilots.
6. Time human review. Target under 40 minutes after brief two.
7. Keep or kill.

## Risks and mitigations

- Hallucinated changes: force citations; human review rejects anything without a link.
- Scope creep into always-on monitoring: sell the brief, not the platform. Weekly retainer is still a brief.
- Overlap with generic "agent monitoring" tools: differentiate on cited one-page output + one action, scoped to 3–5 rivals, delivered by a human-in-the-loop desk.
- Legal/privacy: only public pages and posts. No scraping behind logins, no customer data beyond the competitor list they supply.
- Low willingness to pay: price test early; the public sample is the demand signal.
