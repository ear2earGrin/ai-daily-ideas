## Concept

Sell a **weekly problem-intent reply pack** for one offer the seller already knows how to fulfill.

The customer (indie hacker, freelancer, or 1-3 person studio) uploads:

- a one-page offer note: who they help, the exact problem sentence, what they refuse, price band, proof links
- optional: last 10 closed jobs or testimonials
- optional: accounts / subreddits they already watch
- a "do not contact" list (competitors, prior clients mid-fight, their own alts)

Agents plus one human operator return within 24 hours:

1. A ranked intent table (8-20 rows): platform, handle, post date, quoted problem sentence, fit (`hot / maybe / no`), why, suggested angle.
2. A duplicate / same-person map across X and Reddit.
3. A do-not-touch list: job-seeking spam, "build this for free," regulated medical/legal asks, already-replied threads.
4. Five ready-to-paste first replies (owner posts; agents do not).
5. Two "skip this week" notes so the seller does not feel obliged to farm 20 threads.
6. A source appendix. Every row cites a post URL + excerpt or `not-in-record`.

This is **not** auto-DM, **not** an Upwork bidder, and **not** a content calendar. It is a clerk that turns public complaints into a short reply queue.

## Target user

- Primary buyer: EU/UK/US solo sellers with one clear offer (automation setup, niche report, trade-adjacent software, freelance specialty) who already close work when a human conversation starts.
- Urgent pain: they scroll X for 40 minutes, bookmark six posts, reply to none, then buy another lead tool.
- Existing workaround: TweetDeck columns, Reddit search, or a VA who pastes generic "saw your post" DMs.

## Why this works

- Market signal: today's builder feed treats "which loop first" as the product question. Intent listening is the loop that still needs a human voice and does not collide with the catalog's invoice, dispute, or content-pack desks.
- Agent advantage: search + rank + extract the problem sentence; refuse to invent that the poster asked for a vendor.
- Solo-operator advantage: one person who has sold the offer can tell a tire-kicker from a budgeted complaint. Judgment is the product; the model is the clerk.

2. Demand test: post one anonymized sample pack on X and in 3 indie-hacker / freelancer rooms. DM 25 people who posted "need clients," "marketing agent," or "who do I even reply to." Offer the first 5 packs at 49 / 24h.
3. Success bar: 5 serious replies and 2 paid packs in 14 days. Kill if 25 outbound touches produce zero deposits.

## Execution plan (one person + agents)

**Agent 1 — Intake.** Normalize the offer note. Extract problem sentences, refusals, price band, proof URLs. Refuse to invent a second offer.

**Agent 2 — Listener.** Search X and Reddit (plus optional HN) for the problem sentences and close paraphrases from the last 7 days. Store URL, timestamp, excerpt.

**Agent 3 — Ranker.** Score `hot / maybe / no` against the offer and refusals. Mark already-replied and do-not-touch. Never claim the poster wants to buy.

**Agent 4 — Deduper.** Stitch the same human across platforms when handles or URLs match.

**Agent 5 — Pack drafter.** Fill the table and five replies. Every cell cites a source id or `not-in-record`. Refuse to send the replies.

**Human operator.** Kill creepy or salesy lines, drop false-positive "I have this problem" memes, export PDF + markdown. The model does not post.

Stack to start: coding + browser agents, X/Reddit search, one LLM, Stripe Payment Link, a mailbox. No product UI until the fifth paid job. No auto-follow, no scraped emails.

## Risks and mitigations

- Creepy outreach / platform bans: v1 never DMs, never scrapes emails, never auto-posts. Replies are public-thread first. Owner pastes.
- False intent: if the post is a joke, a hiring post, or "I already hired someone," mark `no` and say why.
- Overlap with cited signal pack: if they want *their* weekly posts written, send them to 2026-09-05. This pack is other people's problem posts.
- Regulated asks (health, legal, kids, weapons): drop the row; do not draft a reply.
- Rate limits / ToS: use public search and user-supplied exports. No credential stuffing.
