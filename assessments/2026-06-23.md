# Assessment: AI-Powered Local Business Website Scout & Builder Agent Swarm

**Idea:** [ideas/2026-06-23.md](../ideas/2026-06-23.md)

## Verdict

Revisit only with a different wedge. The lead-generation half is excellent — Google Maps
hands you a complete, contactable list of every prospect on earth, which is the best
distribution of any idea in this catalog. The selling half is the most commoditized service
in existence, and the delivery mechanism as described (automated cold outreach at volume)
carries the worst compliance profile of the five: CAN-SPAM and GDPR on the messaging,
Google Places terms on the data, and domain reputation risk on top. Great scout, terrible
thing to be selling.

## Viability

- `pain_intensity` 3 — Inverted from what the idea assumes. The local business with no
  website has been fine without one for years; that is *why* they have no website. The
  urgency belongs to the agency buying leads, not the restaurant. Scored for the stated
  done-for-you model, where the customer's pain is mild and chronic.
- `buyer_quality` 3 — Local SMBs genuinely do pay $300-500 for a website — it is an
  established market with a known price. They are also slow to decide, hard to reach by
  phone, and prone to going dark mid-project. Real budget, expensive to collect.
- `reachability` 5 — The best score in the entire catalog and the genuine insight in this
  idea. Google Maps and local directories give you an exhaustive, filterable, geographically
  targeted list of qualified prospects with contact details. Nothing else here comes close
  to that targeting precision.
- `monetization_clarity` 4 — $300-500 per site is publicly benchmarked and universally
  understood by both sides. Docked one point because it is one-off revenue: you are back at
  zero every month unless you attach hosting or maintenance.
- `differentiation` 1 — The lowest score in the catalog, and it is not close. You would be
  competing with every web agency on the planet, with Wix/Squarespace/GoDaddy AI builders
  that do this for $16/month, and with thousands of identical "AI websites for local
  business" cold-outreach operations that already exist. The idea's own inspiration — X
  posts about autonomous lead gen — is itself evidence of saturation.

**Viability: 64/100.**

## Buildability

- `mvp_simplicity` 4 — Technically the pipeline is straightforward: query places, check for
  a site, generate a mockup, draft an email, queue for review. All well-trodden.
- `data_access` 2 — A real constraint that the idea does not acknowledge. Google Places API
  restricts caching and redistribution of results, scraping Maps directly violates its terms,
  and email discovery for the contacts sits in a grey zone. You can build this compliantly
  via the Places API at cost, but not at the scrape-everything volume the concept implies.
- `ai_fit` 4 — Generating a plausible landing page mockup and personalized outreach copy
  from a business listing is well within current model capability, and the visual mockup is
  a genuinely persuasive sales artifact.
- `low_maintenance` 3 — The scout pipeline is low-upkeep. Every site you sell, however, is
  a hosting and support obligation you carry indefinitely for a one-off $400.
- `low_compliance_risk` 1 — The worst in the catalog. Automated cold outreach at volume
  means CAN-SPAM (US), GDPR and PECR (EU/UK), and CASL (Canada) all apply, with per-message
  penalties; Google Places terms govern the data; and sending domains burn fast enough that
  the infrastructure cost is really a reputation-management cost. This is not a footnote,
  it is the operating risk of the business.

**Buildability: 56/100.**

## Tools needed

**Build:**
- Google Places API or SerpAPI — prospect discovery with real contact data — ~$5/1,000 lookups
- Python plus httpx — site presence and quality checks against each listing — free
- Anthropic or OpenAI API — landing page generation and outreach personalization — ~$0.05/prospect
- Playwright — screenshot the current site and render the proposed mockup — free
- Vercel or Cloudflare Pages — host generated mockups at a shareable link — free tier
- Instantly or Smartlead — sending, inbox rotation, warmup, reply detection — $100/mo
- Supabase — prospect pipeline, outreach state, suppression list — $25/mo
- Stripe — collect the $300-500 — 2.9% + 30¢

**Run:**
- A human approval queue before anything sends — required for both quality and legal defensibility — free
- A permanent suppression and unsubscribe list — legally mandatory, not optional — free
- Domain reputation monitoring — the channel degrades silently and you need to see it — $0-30/mo

## Development plan

If you build this, build the scout and sell the leads. Do not build the swarm.

**Phase 0 — Test the wedge before the pipeline (3 days)**
1. Hand-pick 20 local businesses in one trade in one city with poor or missing websites.
2. Generate mockups for 10 of them by hand and send 10 personalized emails yourself, from your own domain, at human volume.
3. Measure reply rate. Below 5% and the entire premise fails at step one, before any automation exists.
4. Try the same 10 as an *agency* offer — "here are 20 qualified local leads with mockups" — and see which side pays faster.

**Phase 1 — Build the scout, not the swarm (5 days)**
1. Places API query → website presence and quality check → scored prospect list in Supabase.
2. Mockup generation with a hosted shareable preview link per prospect.
3. Outreach drafting into an approval queue. Nothing auto-sends, ever.
4. Add the suppression list and unsubscribe handling before the first real send, not after.

**Phase 2 — Pick a side (4 days)**
1. If SMBs converted: add Stripe, a delivery template, and a hosting plan so the one-off becomes recurring.
2. If agencies converted: drop site delivery entirely and sell the qualified-prospect feed as a subscription. This is the better business — no delivery obligation, recurring revenue, and a buyer who feels actual pain.

Total: 12 days, with a hard decision point after the first three.

## Kill criteria

Stop if hand-sent, hand-personalized outreach to 20 prospects yields under 5% replies —
automation multiplies volume, never conversion, and a bad message sent 10,000 times is just
a faster way to burn your domains. Stop immediately if the compliant data path (paid Places
API, honored suppression list, human-approved sends) makes the unit economics negative,
because the version that works is the version that ignores those, and that one ends in
blocked domains and liability rather than a business.
